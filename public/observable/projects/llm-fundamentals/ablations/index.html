Ablation Analysis

Introduction

Ablations are architectural variations. In the context of the source assignment, these were intended to test some basic variations. We'll to through these and describe what they are and what the expected effect is, then proceed to examining the empirical results.

  • Post-Norm: The post-norm ablation alters the moves the RMSNorm within the transformer block from before the attention computation to after, that is between the attention calcuation and the feedforward step. This is expected to slightly degrate model stability, but not have a major effect.
  • SiLU: The SiLU ablation relaces the more complex SwiGLU with a SiLU, that is, it removes the "gating" componentise product and its associated weights and uses just the "Swish" function, . This is expected to slightly decrease convergence rate and increase final losses, as there are fewer "levers" for backpropagation to "pull".
  • NoPE: A fun name for excluding the RoPE postional encoding mechanism. Given the models are trained with a sequence length of 256, it's not clear how much impact this will have. But overall we expect increased losses.
  • No Norm: Removing RMS Norm steps from the computation. This is the ablation expected to have the greatest impact, as "norm early, norm often" has become the rule of thumb for a reason: the persistent conditioning of the residual stream is known to play a central role in preventing exploding gradients. We expect the model to be unstable.

As expected, in our small model post-norm has little impact on losses, however we see a persistently higher gradient norm relative to baseline. This ablation kept the final RMS Norm before the "language head" up-projection, so the difference is likely due to the un-normed inputs to the attention mechanism increasing gradient sizes, a consequence of having larger variances flowing in to heavy computations tracked by the auto-differentiation compute graph.
